What the principles are for

Architecture decision guide
Adoption
Move from experiments to reusable capability

AI pilots should become governed enterprise services, not isolated tools owned by one team.

Architecture
Create consistent design choices

Teams need repeatable patterns for identity, data, grounding, model access, agent actions, logging, and operations.

Governance
Make ARB review evidence-based

Every use case should present risk classification, architecture, controls, owners, test results, and operating model evidence.

Operations
Run AI as a changing service

AI behavior changes when prompts, models, data, users, or vendors change. Architecture governance continues after go-live.

Risk tier decision model

Low risk

Read-only, internal productivity, no sensitive data, no material decision, no autonomous action. Fast-path review is acceptable when platform controls are already approved.

Medium risk

Uses internal business data, influences work decisions, or touches moderate-sensitivity content. Requires architecture, security, privacy, and operational evidence before pilot.

High risk

Processes regulated or sensitive data, affects people materially, writes to enterprise systems, or uses agentic automation. Requires full ARB approval, senior risk acceptance, and stronger testing.

Prohibited / not approved

Hidden surveillance, unlawful use, autonomous material decisions with no accountable human, unrestricted production write access, or uncontrolled sensitive data access.

Practical rule: keep the first production release read-only or draft-only unless the evidence pack proves identity, action limits, rollback, monitoring, and human approval are working.
Architecture principles

Principles that can be tested in practice

Each principle is written as a decision rule. It must influence design, review, deployment, or operations. A principle that cannot be tested becomes a slogan, not architecture governance.

🏛️

Principle set

AIP-01 to AIP-12

AIP-01 · Business value before technology

Statement: Every AI use case must start with a named business outcome, owner, and success measure.

Put into practice: ARB intake must include baseline process cost, target improvement, user group, expected benefit, and review date.

AIP-02 · Platform-first, not tool-first

Statement: AI capabilities should use approved shared platform services before bespoke stacks are introduced.

Put into practice: Reuse enterprise identity, model gateway, prompt management, retrieval service, logging, CI/CD, and support processes unless an exception is approved.

AIP-03 · Knowledge is governed before retrieval

Statement: AI must retrieve only from approved, owned, classified, and access-controlled sources.

Put into practice: Production RAG requires a source register, owner, classification, retention rule, access test, versioning, and citation/provenance design.

AIP-04 · Human accountability remains visible

Statement: AI may assist decisions, but an accountable human remains responsible for material decisions and actions.

Put into practice: Medium and high-risk workflows require HITL checkpoints, named approvers, approval logging, rejection path, and escalation model.

AIP-05 · Security, privacy and residency by design

Statement: Security, privacy, access control, and data residency must be designed before build, not added after deployment.

Put into practice: Threat model prompts, retrieval, tools, output handling, logging, identity, encryption, private connectivity, and vendor/data transfer boundaries.

AIP-06 · Risk-tiered governance

Statement: Governance must scale with data sensitivity, autonomy, external exposure, and decision impact.

Put into practice: Use fast-path review for low-risk internal read-only use cases; require full ARB and risk acceptance for high-impact or action-taking use cases.

AIP-07 · Transparency and contestability built in

Statement: Users must know when AI is used, what it can and cannot do, and how to challenge or correct harmful outcomes.

Put into practice: Publish user notices, source citations where grounding matters, limitations statements, support path, and correction/appeal workflow for material impact.

AIP-08 · Agents are controlled capability escalation

Statement: Action-taking agents must progress through explicit autonomy gates.

Put into practice: Start at read-only or draft-assist. Require allowlisted tools, least privilege, human approval, rate limits, rollback/compensating controls, kill switch, and action logs before production actions.

AIP-09 · Observability is a production requirement

Statement: No AI system enters production without quality, safety, cost, security, and operational telemetry.

Put into practice: Define dashboards, alert thresholds, owners, retention rules, sampling approach, and incident categories before go-live.

AIP-10 · Workflow integration with controlled entry points

Statement: AI should appear where work happens, but only through controlled and supportable interfaces.

Put into practice: Prefer embedded workflow entry points in M365, ITSM, CRM, ERP, knowledge portals, or approved apps. Avoid unmanaged chatbots and shadow interfaces.

AIP-11 · Interoperability and portability by design

Statement: AI architecture must avoid avoidable lock-in and preserve strategic flexibility.

Put into practice: Keep prompts, eval sets, retrieval configuration, model-routing policy, logs, and architecture decisions exportable where practical. Document exit constraints.

AIP-12 · Lifecycle, change and retirement are mandatory

Statement: AI use cases require ownership from intake through retirement.

Put into practice: Register every production AI use case, define review cadence, revalidate after material changes, and retire unsafe, unused, or unsupported AI services.

Agent autonomy gates

GateCapabilityMinimum control
G0No AI. Human-only process.Document baseline process.
G1Read-only AI: retrieve, summarize, answer.Source register, retrieval access, citations, user notice.
G2Draft assist: AI prepares content, human sends/uses.Human review, output validation, audit metadata.
G3Approved action: AI prepares action, human approves execution.Approval log, least-privilege tool, action audit, rejection path.
G4Controlled automation: bounded action under monitoring.All G3 controls plus rate limit, kill switch, rollback/compensating control, alerting.
G5Exceptional autonomy: high-impact automated action.Senior risk acceptance, independent testing, lifecycle assurance, stricter review cadence.
Do not begin an enterprise AI program at G4 or G5. Start with G1 or G2, measure value and behavior, then increase autonomy only when controls are proven.
Operate and improve

Run AI as an enterprise service

AI operations must cover quality, safety, cost, security, support, model/data changes, and business value. Go-live is not the end of architecture governance.

Material change triggers

Model change

New model version, new provider, changed model parameters, changed safety filter, fine-tune update, fallback change.

Data change

New source, changed classification, index rebuild, changed retention, major source content update, new data geography.

Action change

New tool, new write capability, new API scope, new approval bypass, expanded user group, higher autonomy gate.

Risk change

New regulation, new affected user group, new external exposure, incident, adverse outcome, significant cost or quality degradation.

Architecture teams should review recurring AI incidents as architecture feedback. Repeated prompt failures, unsafe tool calls, or access near-misses usually indicate a pattern or platform gap, not just a support issue.
