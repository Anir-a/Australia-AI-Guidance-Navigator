0.1 The Problem Worth Solving

GovernGlobal's daily operations span 14 countries. Their engagement managers manually summarize client meetings into CRM records. Their accounts payable teams match bank statements row by row in spreadsheets. Their procurement analysts review hundreds of contracts each month without AI assistance. Their field engineers arrive on-site at hospitals with no real-time access to the AI-powered guidance that could help them diagnose equipment faults faster.

None of this is unusual. It is the operational condition of most large professional services firms. What makes GovernGlobal different is that they have decided to change it — systematically, with architecture, not experiments.

The question is not whether AI will transform how we work. The question is whether we build the foundation properly before we build the agents on top of it.

— Anir, Platform & Governance Architect

0.2 What an AI Agent Actually Is

The word "agent" is used imprecisely in most organizations. Before GovernGlobal builds anything, Anir draws a definition on the whiteboard that the whole team agrees to use.

What an AI Agent Is (and Is Not)

0.3 The Five Capability Domains

GovernGlobal's AI transformation is organized into five capability domains. Each one is a structural layer — and like floors in a building, you cannot safely occupy the upper floors without the lower ones being architecturally sound. These five domains form the structure of this book.

🗺
The building analogy: You cannot skip the foundation and build the roof. Every GovernGlobal AI agent depends on a governed data layer, which depends on a coherent adoption strategy, which depends on clear business case construction. This is not a linear process — but the dependencies are real, and they run downward.

0.4 The Central Tension of the Whole Program

Before the first line of agent configuration is written, GovernGlobal's team has a tension that every AI program has. It is productive. It is never fully resolved. The book is about how the team manages it.

⚡ Neon wants to ship

  • Prototype in three days, deployed in a week
  • Learns from production — iteration beats planning
  • Technical debt is a future problem
  • Real value requires real users
"Give me a weekend and I'll have something running."

🏗 Anir wants it right

  • Architecture decisions compound — wrong now costs 10x later
  • GovernGlobal handles patient data — mistakes have legal consequences
  • Technical debt in AI isn't just code debt — it's compliance debt
  • The second agent is always easier if the first was done properly
"Give me a week to design it, and I'll save three months of rework."

Both are right. The book shows how GovernGlobal resolves this tension — not by choosing one or the other, but by building the governance structure that lets Neon ship fast inside boundaries that Anir trusts.

1.1 The Cloud Adoption Framework — A Sequencing Model

The Cloud Adoption Framework for Azure is Microsoft's prescribed methodology1 for moving workloads and building AI capabilities in the cloud. It is not a checklist — it is a sequencing model. Each phase produces outputs that are the inputs of the next. Skipping phases doesn't eliminate the work; it moves it to production where it costs ten times more to fix.

CAF for AI — GovernGlobal's Four-Phase Journey
⚡
The cost of skipping Ready: Neon ships an agent in week two that connects to GovernGlobal's SharePoint knowledge base. Three weeks later, Supra discovers the SharePoint library contains unclassified documents including patient health records from a past project. The agent has been summarizing protected health information to users who weren't authorized to see it. This is not a Neon failure — it is an architecture failure. The Ready phase exists to prevent it.

1.2 The Center of Excellence — Three Shapes, Three Stages

A Center of Excellence is not a team. It is a function that changes shape as organizational AI maturity grows. The danger is keeping the same shape past its useful life. A CoE that doesn't evolve becomes the bottleneck it was created to prevent.

CoE Operating Model Evolution — GovernGlobal's Journey

GovernGlobal's legal division built a shadow AI tool using a free-tier subscription because the CoE queue had a seven-week wait time. The tool accessed client contract data with no sensitivity labels, no audit trail, and no governance. That is what happens when a centralized CoE stays Stage 1 too long.

— Anir, recounting the incident that forced CoE evolution

2.1 Grounding — The Mechanism That Makes AI Trustworthy

Without grounding, an AI agent speaks from its training data — which is vast, general, and impossible to audit. With grounding, the agent speaks from your organization's specific data — which is controlled, attributable, and governable. The grounding architecture is the most consequential design decision in any enterprise AI deployment.

Grounded vs Ungrounded AI — GovernGlobal Compliance Agent

2.2 Microsoft Dataverse — The Enterprise AI Fabric

Dataverse is not a database with a connector attached4 to it4. It is Microsoft's unified data platform designed from the ground up to serve AI agents, Dynamics 365 apps, Power Apps, and external models simultaneously from a single, governed source of truth. The distinction matters because it changes which design decisions you make.

2.3 Azure AI Search — When Knowledge Is Distributed

GovernGlobal's knowledge base is not in one system. Policy documents live in SharePoint. Contract data lives in an SQL database. Case management records come from an API. Archived project files are PDFs in blob storage. Azure AI Search solves the heterogeneous data problem by ingesting all source types through configurable indexers and creating one unified semantic index that agents can query.

Azure AI Search — GovernGlobal's Knowledge Unification Architecture
⚠
Incremental vs scheduled indexing: Standard indexing runs on a schedule — every 6, 12, or 24 hours. Incremental indexing triggers when source data changes. For GovernGlobal's procurement agents, a contract status that changes at 9am must be reflected in agent responses at 9:05am — not at 9pm when the nightly index runs. NanaJee insists every data source feeding the search index has incremental indexing enabled. "Stale data is a compliance risk," he says.

2.5 Power BI Semantic Models as AI Sources

GovernGlobal's finance team has spent three years building a certified Power BI semantic model representing client revenue, project margins, and resource allocation — validated by audit, trusted by CFOs across 14 countries. This model should serve as the grounding layer for Bingo's executive briefing agent. But there is a right way and three wrong ways to expose it.

✓

Endorse the Semantic Model6

Registers the model in the AI-ready catalog. Preserves live DirectQuery connectivity. Inherits existing row-level security. The executive agent sees current data with the CFO's access controls intact.

✗

Export to CSV

Creates a static snapshot. Loses live connectivity. Loses row-level security. Executive queries yesterday's data as if it were today's. Compliance risk.

✗

Copy to Dataverse

Creates a raw copy with no DAX measures, no dimensional hierarchies, no business logic. The AI gets numbers without meaning.

✗

Share the Workspace

Makes the workspace visible to additional users. Does not register the model as an AI grounding source. Agent cannot query it.

3.2 Language Models — Three Contracts, Not Three Quality Tiers

Selecting a language model is not about picking the "most capable" option. It is about matching the interaction contract to the business requirement. Each model type provides a different guarantee — and that guarantee is the design decision, not a performance consideration.

NLU Model Selection — GovernGlobal's Three Agents, Three Contracts

A generative NLU model at temperature zero is not deterministic. It is "approximately consistent." That is not the same thing. When a HIPAA auditor asks us to reproduce a specific patient eligibility response for a compliance review, "approximately consistent" is not an acceptable answer. We use NLU with authored dialog nodes for eligibility. Full stop.

— Supra, closing the debate on language model selection for the patient agent

3.3 Orchestration Models — Two Routing Philosophies

Classic Orchestration

"Like a fixed airport gate assignment"
  • Trigger phrases route to specific topics — hardcoded matching
  • Same input always routes to same topic — fully predictable
  • New topics require new trigger phrase sets to be defined
  • Cannot dynamically discover or route to other agents
  • Debug easily: trace which phrase triggered which topic
GovernGlobal use: deterministic compliance response agent where every topic has a fixed authored response

Generative Orchestration

"Like a smart routing system that reads your destination"
  • Topics activated by description of their purpose — no triggers needed
  • New agents auto-discovered based on capability description
  • Routes to other agents dynamically — enables multi-agent composition
  • Handles varied phrasing naturally without exhaustive trigger lists
  • Required when: "topics selected based on description, not trigger phrase"
GovernGlobal use: internal procurement agent routing to specialist sub-agents for HR, Finance, Legal, and Contracts

3.5 Computer Use10 & MCP — Two Different Keys to Locked Rooms

🖱️

Computer Use in Copilot Studio10

The problem it solves: GovernGlobal's government practice submits documents to a legacy benefits portal with no API. The only interface is a web form.

How it works: The AI model evaluates the page layout at runtime — not at design time. When the government portal updates its UI (which it does quarterly without notice), Computer Use re-identifies elements by context and purpose. The automation survives the update.

Use when: no API exists or UI changes frequently

🔌

Model Context Protocol (MCP)

The problem it solves: GovernGlobal's legal team updates their jurisdiction compliance rules weekly. The AI agent needs these updates — but requiring the AI team to update the agent each time is unsustainable.

How it works: An MCP server exposes the legal team's tools through a standardized protocol.11 At runtime, the agent queries the MCP server to discover what tools are currently available, then invokes them. New tools added by the legal team are immediately available to the agent — no redeployment.

Use when: tool catalog changes or is owned by another team

3.6 Multi-Agent Architecture — Earn the Complexity

Abhi proposes a seven-agent architecture for GovernGlobal's government practice. Anir draws it on the whiteboard, then asks: "Which of these agents cannot be a topic in a single agent?" The answer is two. The seven-agent proposal becomes a two-agent architecture. Both requirements are met. The debugging surface is 70% smaller.

4.3 Field Service — Two Users, Two Copilots

GovernGlobal's healthcare equipment maintenance practice has dispatchers who coordinate field engineers from an office (Outlook + Teams), and field engineers who visit hospitals to service medical equipment on site (Field Service mobile app)14. Same organization. Two different users. Two completely different Copilot integrations.

📧 Copilot in Outlook — For Dispatchers

  • Drafts service assignment communications
  • Summarizes correspondence threads from engineers and clients
  • Prioritizes work orders by urgency and location
  • User: Back-office dispatcher with full screen access

📱 Copilot in Field Service — For Engineers

  • Work-order summaries on mobile app before arriving on site
  • Suggested resolution steps based on fault code
  • Relevant knowledge articles for the specific equipment model
  • User: Field engineer in a hospital — often hands-full, no desk access
⚠
The anti-pattern Kutub almost made: He configured Copilot in Customer Service for the field engineers. Copilot in Customer Service supports back-office case agents working tickets in the Customer Service workspace — it does not extend to mobile field technicians. The deployment worked. The adoption failed. Engineers had no AI assistance on site. Gogol received three complaints in week one.

4.6 Contact Center — The Live Agent Hand-Off Architecture

GovernGlobal's citizen services practice runs a contact center for a government client. Citizens call with benefits questions. Most are routine. Some are distressed. The agent must be able to help the first group and safely hand off the second. The hand-off architecture is not optional — it is the safety mechanism.

1

Citizen calls — NLU+ handles voice channel

GovernGlobal's Contact Center agent uses NLU+ — required for voice channel integration with D365 Contact Center. Enhanced context awareness ensures multi-turn benefits conversations are handled correctly.

2

Agent handles routine queries from indexed knowledge

Eligibility questions, document requirements, appointment scheduling — all handled by authored topics with deterministic responses. Supra has approved every authored response.

3

Escalation trigger: "I want to speak to someone"

The Escalation Topic detects the intent. It ends the bot session. It invokes the built-in hand-off action — passing the full conversation transcript, citizen case number, query category, and session ID to the routing layer.

4

Customer Engagement Hub routes to correct queue17

The Hub receives the escalation with full context. It routes to the appropriate agent queue based on query category and citizen language preference. The live agent sees the complete transcript before saying hello.

5

Gogol's ITSM record is created automatically

Every escalation generates an ITSM ticket: escalation reason, session ID, citizen identifier (masked), timestamp, queue route. If the bot's escalation rate spikes, Gogol sees it in his dashboard before anyone else knows. That is the operational governance loop closed.
